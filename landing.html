How Peter Parker became Spider-Man

Spider-Man was created by writer Stan Lee and artist Steve Ditko and first appeared in Amazing Fantasy #15 in 1962. His origin has been retold many times, but the core steps stay the same.

  1. 1

    A student with a head for science

    Peter is a bright, shy high schooler raised by his Uncle Ben and Aunt May. He is more at home in a lab than in a crowd.

  2. 2

    The bite

    During a science demonstration, a radioactive spider bites him. Soon after, he finds he can stick to walls, lift far more than he should, and sense danger before it arrives.

  3. 3

    A costly mistake

    At first Peter uses his powers for fame and money. When he lets a criminal escape, that same criminal later takes his Uncle Ben's life. The loss changes him.

  4. 4

    A promise to do better

    Peter commits to using his abilities to help others. He builds his own web-shooters, makes a mask and suit, and starts patrolling the city.

Quick facts

Real name: Peter Benjamin Parker

Home: Queens, New York City

Day jobs: Photographer, science student, teacher, inventor (varies by era)

Known for: Humor in the middle of a fight, and trying to balance hero work with a normal life

Creators: Stan Lee and Steve Ditko

What Spider-Man can do

His powers come from the bite, but his brain and his habits matter just as much.

🧗

Wall-crawling

Peter can cling to almost any surface with his hands and feet, which lets him climb buildings, ceilings and bridges with ease.

🔔

Spider-sense

A tingling warning that tells him when danger is close, often a split second before it hits. It gives him time to dodge, duck or react.

💪

Strength and speed

He can lift vehicles, leap long distances and move faster than an ordinary person can follow. His reflexes are just as sharp.

🤸

Agility and balance

Flips, rolls and perfect balance on thin ledges are part of his routine. It is a big part of how he fights without being hit.

🕸️

Web-shooters

In the comics, Peter invents wrist devices that fire strong synthetic webbing. Some adaptations give him organic webbing instead, but the swinging stays the same.

🧪

Scientific genius

Peter designs his own gadgets, mixes his own web fluid and solves problems like an engineer. His mind is as much a weapon as his strength.
